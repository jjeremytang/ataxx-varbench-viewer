# V4-01 Formal Results — Position-Level Selective Deepening

## 1. Research question

V4-01 evaluates whether the locked V3-03 volatility detector can guide position-level selective deepening from d4 to d6 and obtain a better strength/cost tradeoff than fixed-depth search.

Primary question:

> Can a detector-guided policy become stronger than fixed d4 while using materially less search cost than fixed d6?

The primary selective policy is `PSV-20`, using the validation-derived fixed threshold `0.500931073443522`. The detector is used only for ply 6–30. Thresholds were frozen before formal game outcomes and were not adjusted afterward.

Protocol: `docs/v4_01_position_selective_deepening_protocol.md`

## 2. Formal experiment design

- Board: standard 7×7 Ataxx
- Obstacles: none
- Frozen openings: 50
- Opening prefix: exactly 4 random legal plies under the frozen opening procedure
- Matchups:
  - `PSV-20 vs P4`: 100 games
  - `PSV-20 vs P6`: 100 games
- Each opening is played twice per matchup with colors swapped.
- Total formal games: 200
- Build: Release
- Formal raw-result commit: `629337722514da0fc478a8804018b3b79b77497e`
- Formal request commit: `a4cec8e1a04d36f29077328be8f6cb66d57ea137`
- Raw result path: `results/v4_01_formal_primary/`
- Validation manifest: `results/v4_01_formal_primary/run_manifest.json`
- Analysis summary: `results/v4_01_formal_primary/analysis_summary.json`

Validation result:

- 200 / 200 games validated
- 15,485 move rows validated
- 188 natural terminations
- 12 repetition terminations
- opening, threshold and model-selection provenance matched the frozen artifacts

## 3. Playing-strength results

Score rate is defined as:

`(wins + 0.5 × draws) / games`

| Matchup | PSV wins | Draws | PSV losses | PSV score rate | 95% paired bootstrap CI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PSV-20 vs P4 | 56 | 6 | 38 | **59.0%** | **51.0%–67.0%** |
| PSV-20 vs P6 | 18 | 6 | 76 | **21.0%** | **13.5%–29.0%** |

The confidence interval is bootstrapped over the 50 color-swapped opening pairs, not over individual games.

### 3.1 PSV-20 vs P4

PSV-20 scored 59.0% against fixed d4. The paired bootstrap interval is 51.0%–67.0%.

This supports the conclusion that the implemented selective-deepening policy improves actual playing strength over fixed d4 under the tested setup.

Paired-opening scores for PSV across the 50 openings:

| PSV score across the color-swapped pair | Opening count |
|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 4 |
| 0.5 | 3 |
| 1.0 | 27 |
| 1.5 | 3 |
| 2.0 | 13 |

### 3.2 PSV-20 vs P6

PSV-20 scored only 21.0% against fixed d6, with a paired bootstrap interval of 13.5%–29.0%.

This does **not** support a claim that PSV-20 has playing strength close to fixed d6.

Paired-opening scores for PSV across the 50 openings:

| PSV score across the color-swapped pair | Opening count |
|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 29 |
| 0.5 | 4 |
| 1.0 | 14 |
| 1.5 | 2 |
| 2.0 | 1 |

The large number of 0-point opening pairs against P6 shows that the gap is not explained only by color assignment.

PSV score rate by color:

| Matchup | PSV as Red | PSV as Blue |
|---|---:|---:|
| vs P4 | 64% | 54% |
| vs P6 | 31% | 11% |

The color effect is substantial, especially against P6. This validates the decision to use color-swapped paired games for the formal experiment.

## 5. Search-cost results

### 5.1 PSV-20 vs P4

| Policy | Nodes / game | Wall time / game | Mean move time | Median move time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PSV-20 | 3,680,027.97 | 40.193 s | 1.040 s | 0.133 s |
| P4 | 575,290.70 | 5.664 s | 0.146 s | 0.118 s |

Cost ratios:

- PSV / P4 nodes = **6.40×**
- PSV / P4 wall time = **7.10×**

Therefore the improvement from 50% baseline expectation to 59% score rate is not free: the current implementation is substantially more expensive than fixed d4.

### 5.2 PSV-20 vs P6

| Policy | Nodes / game | Wall time / game | Mean move time | Median move time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PSV-20 | 3,077,744.97 | 26.601 s | 0.687 s | 0.124 s |
| P6 | 23,020,993.42 | 290.786 s | 7.518 s | 4.697 s |

Cost ratios:

- PSV / P6 nodes = **13.37%**
- PSV / P6 wall time = **9.15%**

Thus PSV-20 is dramatically cheaper than fixed d6, but its 21% score rate against P6 shows that the saved computation also corresponds to a large remaining strength gap.

Node count should be treated as the more architecture-independent cost proxy. Wall-clock values are useful practical measurements but were collected across parallel GitHub-hosted runners and therefore should not be interpreted as precision hardware benchmarks.

## 6. Detector behavior during formal games

Across all 200 games:

- PSV moves: **7,738**
- detector-eligible moves: **2,336**
- detector-ineligible moves: **5,402**
- eligible fraction of PSV moves: **30.19%**
- d6 calls: **554**
- realized deepen rate among eligible moves: **23.72%**
- realized deepen rate among all PSV moves: **7.16%**
- d6 calls per game: **2.77**
- deepened moves where d6 changed the d4 best move: **376**
- changed fraction among deepened moves: **67.87%**

The `PSV-20` name denotes the validation target fraction. It does not force exactly 20% of future game positions to deepen. The fixed threshold produced a 23.72% realized eligible deepen fraction in formal games, indicating a distribution shift between the validation positions and encountered game positions.

The fact that 67.87% of triggered d6 searches changed the d4 move shows that deepening was usually decision-relevant rather than merely recomputing the same move.

## 7. Offline fixed-threshold sanity check

Before formal games, the frozen `PSV-20` threshold was applied to the locked V3-03 test predictions without any threshold adjustment.

Results on 600 test positions:

- selected: 106 / 600 = **17.67%**
- captured d4→d6 flips: 57 / 207
- flip recall: **27.54%**
- flip precision: **53.77%**
- recall lift over realized random selection: **1.56×**
- move agreement with fixed d6: **75.0%**
- policy node proxy including d2 / fixed d6: **26.95%**

This sanity check supported continuing to formal game-strength evaluation, but it was not used to modify the threshold.

## 8. Main interpretation

V4-01 supports the following statement:

> Within the tested standard 7×7 Ataxx setting and the detector's trained ply 6–30 domain, volatility-aware position-level selective deepening can improve playing strength over fixed d4 while using far less search cost than fixed d6.

However, V4-01 also shows a clear limitation:

> The current PSV-20 policy remains far weaker than fixed d6. A 21% score rate against P6 does not support the claim that the policy achieves d6-like playing strength.

The most compact description of the observed tradeoff is:

- compared with P4: **stronger, but much more expensive**;
- compared with P6: **much cheaper, but much weaker**.

Therefore V4-01 demonstrates that selective allocation is useful, but it does not yet solve the resource-allocation problem well enough to replace fixed d6.

## 9. What should not be claimed

The current results do not establish that:

- PSV-20 is close to fixed d6 strength;
- the 20% validation target is the optimal budget;
- d6 is ground truth or perfect play;
- the detector is valid outside ply 6–30;
- the same results generalize to other board sizes or obstacle layouts;
- branch-level selective deepening is superior before it is separately tested.

The primary threshold must not be retuned on these same formal outcomes and then presented as if it were still the preregistered primary experiment.

## 10. Research implication and next step

The next question is no longer simply whether selective deepening works. V4-01 shows that it can improve over d4, but the gap to d6 remains large.

The next diagnostic task is therefore to determine **why**:

1. the detector may miss many positions where d4 and d6 would disagree;
2. the position-level budget may be too low for the desired strength target;
3. whole-position d4→d6 deepening may be too coarse and may spend nodes on branches that do not need extra depth.

Recommended next task: `V4-02 — Offline Oracle Replay`.

Replay the recorded formal PSV states offline, run d6 on all detector-eligible states, and classify them into:

- deepen + d4/d6 differ: useful trigger;
- deepen + d4/d6 same: unnecessary deepening for move choice;
- no deepen + d4/d6 same: correctly saved work;
- no deepen + d4/d6 differ: missed instability.

This diagnosis should precede any new detector training or branch-level policy implementation.
